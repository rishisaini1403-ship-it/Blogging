# I’m Still Figuring It Out — And That’s the Point

I don't think I have a proper answer when someone asks me, **“So, what do you want to become?”**

A software engineer?

A builder?

An entrepreneur?

Someone who works with AI?

Maybe all of them.

Or maybe none of them.

The truth is, I'm still figuring it out.

And for a long time, I thought that was a problem.

We grow up believing that at some point, everything will become clear. We'll know what we want to do, where we want to go, and who we want to become. School asks us what we want to be. College asks us what job we want. Society asks us what our plan is.

But nobody really tells us what to say when the honest answer is:

**“I don't know yet.”**

I'm currently studying Computer Science, learning how to write code, building projects, experimenting with AI, trying different technologies, and occasionally staring at my screen wondering whether I'm actually getting anywhere.

Some days I feel incredibly motivated.

Other days, I feel like everyone around me is moving faster.

Someone is doing competitive programming.

Someone is getting an internship.

Someone is building a startup.

Someone is contributing to open source.

Someone already seems to know exactly what they're doing.

And then there's me, trying to understand what I actually want.

I've slowly started realizing something, though.

**Life isn't a race where everyone starts at the same place and finishes at the same destination.**

Maybe I'm not behind.

Maybe I'm just on a different part of the road.

## The illusion of having it figured out

One of the strangest things about being young is that almost everyone looks like they know what they're doing.

You look at someone's LinkedIn profile and see internships, projects, achievements, certificates and impressive titles.

You see someone succeeding and you only see the result.

You don't see the nights they doubted themselves.

You don't see the applications they were rejected from.

You don't see the projects that never worked.

You don't see the days when they questioned whether they were good enough.

The internet has made comparison incredibly easy.

A person can be sitting alone in their room and, within five minutes, compare their entire life to hundreds of other people.

I've done it too.

And comparison can make you forget something important:

**You are looking at someone else's chapter while judging yourself for not being at the same page.**

Maybe the real goal isn't to become better than everyone else.

Maybe it's to become slightly better than the person you were yesterday.

## I like building things

There's something satisfying about creating something that didn't exist before.

It doesn't have to be revolutionary.

Sometimes it's just a small application.

A website.

A feature.

An idea that finally works after hours of debugging.

When I build something, I get a strange feeling that I don't get from simply studying theory.

It's the feeling of taking something that exists only inside your head and turning it into something real.

That's probably one of the reasons I'm interested in technology.

Technology isn't just about computers for me.

It's about possibilities.

A problem exists.

You think about it.

You experiment.

You fail.

You change something.

And eventually, maybe, you create a solution.

There is something almost philosophical about that process.

You imagine a different reality, and then you try to build it.

## Failure doesn't feel inspiring while you're experiencing it

We often talk about failure like it's beautiful.

“Failure teaches you.”

“Keep going.”

“Every failure is a lesson.”

Maybe that's true.

But when you're actually failing, it doesn't feel like a lesson.

It feels frustrating.

It feels embarrassing.

Sometimes it makes you question yourself.

I've had moments where something I expected to work simply didn't.

Projects didn't go exactly as planned.

Opportunities didn't work out.

Things I thought would happen didn't happen.

And I've learned that accepting failure doesn't mean pretending it doesn't hurt.

It means allowing yourself to be disappointed without allowing that disappointment to define you.

Maybe failure isn't the opposite of progress.

Maybe sometimes **failure is what progress looks like from the inside.**

## I don't want a perfect life

The older I get, the less interested I am in having a life that looks perfect from the outside.

I want a life that actually feels meaningful from the inside.

I want to work on things I'm curious about.

I want to meet people who challenge the way I think.

I want to build things I'm proud of.

I want to learn things that make me look at the world differently.

I want to fail at things that were worth attempting.

And most importantly, I don't want to become so obsessed with the destination that I forget to experience the journey.

Because there will always be another milestone.

Another semester.

Another internship.

Another job.

Another salary target.

Another achievement.

Another person to compete with.

If happiness is always waiting for the next milestone, then we'll spend our entire lives waiting.

Maybe life isn't about finally reaching a point where everything makes sense.

Maybe life is about becoming comfortable with the fact that it doesn't.

## So, who am I?

I'm a Computer Science student.

I'm someone learning to code and build.

I'm interested in AI, technology, and the endless possibilities that come with them.

I'm ambitious, but I'm also uncertain.

I'm curious, but I don't have all the answers.

I have goals, but they change.

I have plans, but life keeps rewriting them.

And honestly, I'm okay with that now.

I don't know exactly where I'll be five years from now.

Maybe I'll be a software engineer.

Maybe I'll build a company.

Maybe I'll work in AI.

Maybe I'll discover something completely different.

I don't know.

But perhaps that's what makes this stage of life interesting.

**I'm not supposed to have the entire story figured out yet.**

I'm only supposed to write the next page.

And then the next one.

And then the next.

So, if you're reading this while feeling lost, confused, behind, or uncertain about your own future, maybe you're not failing either.

Maybe you're simply becoming.

And becoming is messy.

It's slow.

It's uncomfortable.

Sometimes it feels like nothing is happening.

But underneath all that uncertainty, something is changing.

**You are becoming someone you've never been before.**

And maybe that's enough for today.
